Status: reusable pre-writing evidence foundation

Coverage: 10 parent topics and 60 planned articles in [TOPICAL_AUTHORITY.md](TOPICAL_AUTHORITY.md) and [ARTICLE_CATALOG.md](ARTICLE_CATALOG.md)

Writers must cite the original source, not this file. This research map does not replace a licensed standard, approved project documents, an engineering calculation, a laboratory report, a product data sheet, a site safety plan, or competent professional review.

## Purpose

This file gives the whole Concrete.co.id catalog one shared ground-truth layer. It reduces repeated discovery while preserving limits: a standards catalogue record proves a document's identity and public scope, not every requirement inside it; a test method does not prove a real batch passed; and general guidance does not approve a project-specific design or work method.

## Evidence grades

- **A-ID:** current Indonesian law or official BSN standard record.
- **A-INT:** current official international standards-body record.
- **B:** government or intergovernmental safety/technical guidance.
- **C:** project, manufacturer, laboratory, or supplier evidence that must be obtained for the exact case.
- **Gate:** a decision remains open until full documents, current project facts, testing, or competent review are available.

### KR-02 — Concrete specification requires more than a grade label

- **Sources:** [ISO 22965-1:2007 concrete specification](https://www.iso.org/standard/41260.html), [SNI 6880:2016 structural concrete specification record](https://pesta.bsn.go.id/produk/detail/10771-sni68802016).
- **Grade:** A-INT and A-ID catalogue doorway.
- **Purpose:** Ground specification, supplier information exchange, and the distinction between shorthand strength labels and a complete requirement.
- **Summary:** ISO 22965-1 covers methods of specifying concrete and information exchange for site-mixed, ready-mixed, and precast production, while its public scope explicitly excludes several special materials and worker-safety requirements.
- **Grounded facts:** Application, constituent requirements, fresh and hardened performance, production route, information exchange, local conditions, and acceptance evidence are separate specification inputs.
- **Incorporation:** Use in `CON-01`, `CON-02`, `CON-03`, and `CON-10` for specification-reading, ordering, scope-comparison, and document articles.
- **Limits/recheck:** Obtain the current full governing SNI/contract documents before quoting clauses, categories, tolerances, test frequencies, or acceptance criteria.

### KR-03 — Structural concrete decisions belong to a complete design basis

- **Sources:** [SNI 2847:2019 concrete structural requirements record](https://pesta.bsn.go.id/produk/detail/12731-sni28472019), [SNI 1727:2020 minimum design loads record](https://pesta.bsn.go.id/produk/detail/12927-sni17272020).
- **Grade:** A-ID catalogue records.
- **Purpose:** Ground reinforced-concrete, cover, joints, formwork, temporary condition, and modification boundaries.
- **Summary:** The official records identify separate governing documents for concrete structural requirements and design loads. Material identity or nominal concrete grade alone cannot establish a member's adequacy.
- **Grounded facts:** Loads, geometry, reinforcement, supports, openings, exposure, fire, serviceability, temporary stages, construction sequence, and approved details can interact.
- **Incorporation:** Use in `CON-01` and `CON-04`, and as the structural gate for cutting/coring in `CON-09`.
- **Limits/recheck:** No sizing, capacity, reinforcement, cover, joint location, stripping time, drilling/cutting, or structural acceptance without the current full standards and project engineer approval.

### KR-04 — Concrete performance depends on identifiable constituents and controlled production

- **Sources:** [ISO 22965-2:2007 production and compliance catalogue](https://www.iso.org/committee/49906/x/catalogue/), [BSN concrete and concrete-product catalogue](https://pesta.bsn.go.id/produk/by_ics/21?ics_no=91&key=).
- **Grade:** A-INT and A-ID catalogue doorways.
- **Purpose:** Ground constituent roles, mix design, trial mix, batching, production, and evidence chains.
- **Summary:** Concrete outcomes depend on the actual cementitious materials, aggregates, water, admixtures, proportions, mixing, placement, compaction, temperature, curing, age, and test methods.
- **Grounded facts:** A proposed mix, trial result, batch record, fresh test, specimen result, and the condition of the installed concrete are different evidence.
- **Incorporation:** Use throughout `CON-02`, `CON-03`, `CON-05`, and the production portions of `CON-09`.
- **Limits/recheck:** No universal recipe, water-binder ratio, workability, strength development, curing duration, or compliance claim without exact materials and governing project evidence.

### KR-05 — Mixing water has its own specification boundary

- **Sources:** [SNI 7974:2018 mixing-water specification record](https://pesta.bsn.go.id/produk/detail/13897-sni79742018), [ISO 22965-1:2007 scope](https://www.iso.org/standard/41260.html).
- **Grade:** A-ID and A-INT.
- **Purpose:** Ground articles about mixing water, field water addition, and information exchange.
- **Summary:** The BSN record identifies a specific standard for water used in hydraulic-cement concrete production; it does not establish that any available water source is acceptable.
- **Grounded facts:** Water source, intended use, test evidence, batch identity, authorized adjustment, and curing-water compatibility are separate questions.
- **Incorporation:** Use in `CON-02-02`, `CON-02-04`, `CON-03-03`, and `CON-09-04`.
- **Limits/recheck:** Obtain the full applicable standard, supplier procedure, project specification, and actual water evidence before stating limits or authorizing an addition.

### KR-06 — Sampling and specimens require traceability

- **Sources:** [SNI 4810:2018 field specimen record](https://pesta.bsn.go.id/produk/detail/13475-48102018), [ISO/IEC 17025:2017 laboratory competence](https://www.iso.org/standard/66912.html).
- **Grade:** A-ID and A-INT.
- **Purpose:** Ground sampling, specimen preparation/care, laboratory competence, report reading, and nonconformity articles.
- **Summary:** Field specimen practice and laboratory competence are distinct. A competent laboratory still needs the correct sample identity, method, condition, project criteria, and reporting chain.
- **Grounded facts:** Concrete batch, sample, specimen, curing history, test method, test age, result, laboratory scope, acceptance rule, and final disposition must not be conflated.
- **Incorporation:** Use across `CON-05` and in `CON-03-03`, `CON-03-06`, and `CON-10-03`.
- **Limits/recheck:** Do not invent specimen shape conversion, frequency, acceptance limit, or disposition; obtain current full methods and project quality documents.

### KR-07 — Quality management links records but does not create technical acceptance

- **Sources:** [ISO 9001:2015 quality management](https://www.iso.org/standard/62085.html), [ISO/IEC 17025:2017](https://www.iso.org/standard/66912.html).
- **Grade:** A-INT.
- **Purpose:** Ground ITP, hold-point, nonconformity, release, and handover logic.
- **Summary:** A controlled system can connect requirements, responsibilities, records, nonconformities, corrective actions, and retained evidence, but certification alone does not prove a concrete batch or structure conforms.
- **Grounded facts:** Procedure approval, inspection, witness, result, NCR, disposition, repair, release, and handover are separate control events.
- **Incorporation:** Use in `CON-05-06`, `CON-07`, and `CON-10-03`–`CON-10-04`.
- **Limits/recheck:** Obtain the current project quality plan, ITP, authority matrix, laboratory scope, and original records before naming a hold point or concluding acceptance.

### KR-08 — Ready-mix is an information and logistics chain

- **Sources:** [ISO 22965-1:2007](https://www.iso.org/standard/41260.html), [ISO 22965-2 catalogue entry](https://www.iso.org/committee/49906/x/catalogue/).
- **Grade:** A-INT.
- **Purpose:** Ground ordering, site readiness, delivery tickets, fresh observations, placement, and coordination.
- **Summary:** Specifier-to-producer information, production, dispatch, transport, receipt, placement, sampling, and acceptance are connected but accountable stages.
- **Grounded facts:** An order, batch record, delivery ticket, arrival observation, sample, specimen result, and placed element describe different stages.
- **Incorporation:** Use across `CON-03` and for cost/scope articles in `CON-10`.
- **Limits/recheck:** Current supplier capability, travel time, allowable adjustment, pumpability, placement window, price, and availability require a dated quotation and project method.

### KR-09 — Precast suitability includes temporary stages and split responsibilities

- **Sources:** [SNI 8977:2021 precast architectural design-responsibility record](https://pesta.bsn.go.id/produk/detail/13482-sni89772021), [SNI 2847:2019](https://pesta.bsn.go.id/produk/detail/12731-sni28472019).
- **Grade:** A-ID catalogue records.
- **Purpose:** Ground product selection, design responsibility, transport, lifting, erection, connections, and tolerance boundaries.
- **Summary:** Precast work can divide responsibility among designer, producer, transporter, erector, supervisor, and owner; units also experience different support and load conditions before permanent service.
- **Grounded facts:** Product geometry, mass, drawing, material evidence, lifting insert, transport support, erection survey, connection record, and completed-system acceptance prove different layers.
- **Incorporation:** Use across `CON-07` and for precast comparison in `CON-01-04`.
- **Limits/recheck:** No capacity, lifting point, stack support, rigging, connection, tolerance, installation, or design-authority claim without exact unit and project documents.

### KR-10 — Lifting and construction safety rules must be checked as a current legal set

- **Sources:** [Permenaker No. 8 Tahun 2020](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Home/Details/163270/permenaker-no-8-tahun-2020), [Permenaker No. 11 Tahun 2026](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/351282/permenaker-no-11-tahun-2026), [Permen PUPR No. 10 Tahun 2021 SMKK](https://jdih.pu.go.id/detail-dokumen/PermenPUPR-nomor-10-tahun-2021-Pedoman-Sistem-Manajemen-Keselamatan-Konstruksi).
- **Grade:** A-ID law.
- **Purpose:** Ground lifting, equipment, site roles, inspection, and construction-safety governance.
- **Summary:** The 2026 supervision rule partially revokes specified provisions of several K3 regulations, including the 2020 lifting/transport rule. Legal claims therefore require consolidated, date-specific review rather than copying an older summary.
- **Grounded facts:** Equipment identity/configuration, inspection/testing, competent roles, load/unit data, ground/support, access, exclusion, weather, communication, rescue, and approved method are distinct controls.
- **Incorporation:** Use in `CON-07-04`–`CON-07-05`, `CON-09`, and construction-governance portions of `CON-10`.
- **Limits/recheck:** Recheck the current official consolidated law immediately before publication; never turn an article into an equipment operating, lifting, traffic, or site safety plan.

### KR-11 — Concrete dust and wet concrete are occupational hazards

- **Sources:** [ILO safety and health in construction code](https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/wcmsp5/groups/public/%40ed_dialogue/%40sector/documents/normativeinstrument/wcms_861584.pdf), [ILO construction OSH training material](https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/wcmsp5/groups/public/%40ed_dialogue/%40sector/documents/instructionalmaterial/wcms_161770.pdf).
- **Grade:** B, intergovernmental guidance; not Indonesian law.
- **Purpose:** Ground hazard awareness for cutting, coring, demolition, dust, cement contact, and control planning.
- **Summary:** Concrete cutting can create respirable silica-containing dust, while cement and wet concrete can create respiratory and skin hazards. Controls must be selected for the actual task and exposure.
- **Grounded facts:** Elimination/substitution, isolation, engineering controls, work practices, hygiene, exposure assessment, and suitable PPE are different control layers.
- **Incorporation:** Use in `CON-09-05`–`CON-09-06` and relevant repair/demolition articles.
- **Limits/recheck:** Do not convert ILO guidance into Indonesian exposure limits or a complete control plan; obtain current Indonesian law, SDS, task assessment, and competent HSE review.

### KR-12 — Durability is exposure- and evidence-dependent

- **Sources:** [ISO 16204:2012 service-life design](https://www.iso.org/standard/55862.html), [ISO 22040:2021 concrete-structure lifecycle management](https://www.iso.org/standard/72390.html).
- **Grade:** A-INT.
- **Purpose:** Ground durability, service-life, inspection, maintenance, and lifecycle planning.
- **Summary:** Durability verification considers known or foreseeable deterioration actions, while lifecycle management spans planning, design, execution, use, and end of life.
- **Grounded facts:** Exposure, design assumptions, material/production evidence, construction records, inspection baseline, deterioration mechanism, maintenance action, and actual performance are distinct.
- **Incorporation:** Use in `CON-02`, `CON-04-02`, `CON-06`, and `CON-10-04`–`CON-10-06`.
- **Limits/recheck:** No universal service life, maintenance interval, corrosion threshold, or durability result without the structure's environment, condition, full criteria, and competent review.

### KR-13 — Assessment must precede consequential repair decisions

- **Sources:** [ISO 16311-1:2024 maintenance and repair principles](https://www.iso.org/standard/86446.html), [ISO 16311-2:2024 assessment of existing concrete structures](https://www.iso.org/standard/79786.html).
- **Grade:** A-INT.
- **Purpose:** Ground crack, honeycomb, leakage, corrosion, spalling, assessment, and repair articles.
- **Summary:** The standards distinguish deterioration from short-duration damage and describe assessment as a structured precursor to consequential maintenance or repair decisions.
- **Grounded facts:** Symptom, history, exposure, survey limit, testing, mechanism hypothesis, safety consequence, repair objective, design, execution, acceptance, and monitoring are separate.
- **Incorporation:** Use across `CON-06`, in surface diagnosis under `CON-08`, and lifecycle decisions under `CON-10`.
- **Limits/recheck:** A photograph or generic symptom cannot establish cause, capacity, remaining life, repair material, or safe continued use.

### KR-14 — Surface finishes are systems tied to the substrate and acceptance conditions

- **Sources:** [ISO 22965-1:2007 concrete scope](https://www.iso.org/standard/41260.html), [ISO 16311-1:2024 maintenance and repair principles](https://www.iso.org/standard/86446.html).
- **Grade:** A-INT foundation plus C project/product evidence.
- **Purpose:** Ground floor, polished, trowelled, decorative, coating, cleaning, and mock-up decisions.
- **Summary:** A surface outcome can depend on concrete substrate, moisture, flatness, timing, finishing process, curing, exposure, applied system, viewing conditions, use, and care.
- **Grounded facts:** A sample, mock-up, product data sheet, substrate test, method statement, batch/area record, acceptance criteria, and maintenance instruction prove different things.
- **Incorporation:** Use across `CON-08` and for curing/quality interfaces in `CON-05` and `CON-09`.
- **Limits/recheck:** Obtain exact product TDS/SDS, substrate criteria, mock-up agreement, environment, installer method, and warranty before promising appearance, adhesion, slip, chemical, or maintenance performance.

### KR-15 — Environmental claims require a declared lifecycle boundary

- **Sources:** [ISO 13315-4:2017 environmental design of concrete structures](https://www.iso.org/cms/live/live/en/sites/isoorg/contents/data/standard/06/65/66540.html), [ISO 22040:2021 lifecycle management](https://www.iso.org/standard/72390.html), [PUPR instruction on non-OPC cement](https://jdih.pu.go.id/internal/assets/assets/produk/InmenPUPR/2020/09/InmenPUPR04-2020.pdf).
- **Grade:** A-INT and A-ID policy context.
- **Purpose:** Ground environmental-claim verification, lifecycle trade-offs, repair/reuse, and procurement.
- **Summary:** Environmental design can use lifecycle assessment or another appropriate method; comparing materials or systems requires consistent function, boundary, data, geography, time, construction, use, maintenance, and end-of-life assumptions.
- **Grounded facts:** Cement type, mix quantity, declared unit, transport, construction waste, design life, maintenance, structural efficiency, reuse, demolition, and disposal can change the result.
- **Incorporation:** Use in `CON-10-05`–`CON-10-06`, and as a boundary in constituent and procurement articles.
- **Limits/recheck:** No “green,” low-carbon, recycled-content, or percentage-reduction claim without a current matched EPD/LCA or equivalent project evidence and transparent boundary.

## Cross-catalog fact bank

- A grade label, delivery ticket, slump observation, specimen result, and installed-structure assessment answer different questions.
- A standards catalogue record confirms identity, public scope, and status shown on that page; it is not the licensed full text.
- A test method defines how evidence is produced. It does not prove an untested batch, product, or structure passed.
- Laboratory competence does not select the project's sampling frequency, specimen, acceptance rule, or disposition authority.
- Adding water, changing materials, cutting reinforcement, drilling concrete, moving joints, or modifying temporary works can alter more than appearance and requires the correct approval path.
- A crack, leak, spall, stain, honeycomb area, or low test result starts an investigation; it does not finish one.
- Product size and nominal strength do not prove precast suitability, transportability, lift safety, connection fit, or installed-system performance.
- “Per cubic metre” is not a complete commercial scope; access, pumping, waiting, testing, waste, labour, formwork, reinforcement, curing, finishing, and risk allocation may remain outside it.
- Environmental comparisons need the same function, lifecycle boundary, data quality, geography, time, and service assumptions.
